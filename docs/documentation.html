Documentation

Unit of observation

One row per NYSE trading session, from 1960-01-04 to 2025-12-31. The session list follows the holiday and closure rules of the NYSE, and every closure is recorded with an identifier and a cause, including the Wednesday closures of the 1968 paperwork crisis and the closures after September 11, 2001. Scheduled opening and closing times are recorded for each session, so early closes are part of the data.

Dating rule

The return of session t runs from the close of session t−1 to the close of session t. An event that occurs on a trading day, at or before the close, belongs to that day's session. Any other event belongs to the next session: events on weekends or holidays, earnings announced after the close, and sports finals played in the evening are assigned to the next session, the first one in which the market can react to them. A macroeconomic release with no recorded time is assigned its usual release time of 8:30. Whenever an official source exists, the date comes from it: the release calendar of the statistical agency, the Federal Reserve for FOMC decisions, the exchange for closures.

Timing

Each column is tagged ex_ante (193 columns) if its value is known before the session opens, as for calendar positions and the dates of scheduled announcements, or realized (215 columns) if it becomes known only during or after the session, as for the weather, disasters or daily uncertainty indices. Realized columns should be lagged by one session in forecasts.

Using the columns as controls

Beyond day effects, the columns can serve as control variables: calendar and announcement indicators in daily return regressions, flags for FOMC or macroeconomic release days in event studies, scheduled releases, holidays and early closes in volatility and liquidity models, and monthly counts of sessions and announcements in monthly regressions. Several columns can describe the same event, so keep one of them in a regression, and use only ex_ante columns, or lagged realized ones, in forecasts.

Versions and updates

The data are updated once a year, in the first quarter, when the year-end data are available. Each update is a new version with its own DOI. A version is never overwritten, and the sources of each version are pinned: every raw download is recorded with its address, the time it was made and its checksum.
