Characteristics of every NYSE trading day since 1960

An open dataset of 408 day characteristics for each of the 16,610 NYSE sessions from 1960 to 2025: the calendar, scheduled macroeconomic and monetary-policy announcements, political, sports and natural events, weather and astronomy, attention, and market conditions. Every event is placed on the first session in which the market could react to it, and every column records where its dates come from.

What the data show

The paper that comes with the dataset, The Cross-Section of Trading Days, uses it to study how average returns differ across types of days.

  • Few types of days earn returns that differ significantly and persistently from those of other days. Apart from calendar effects, no characteristic that is significant in 1960–1969 remains significant in later decades.
  • Strategies that trade on day characteristics earned high risk-adjusted returns until the early 1990s, and much less since.
  • Since 1994, the FOMC decision day is the only announcement day with a significant premium, and this premium has been smaller since 2012.
  • Placing an event on the wrong session can create a day effect that does not exist or erase one that does. The paper derives a formula for the size of this error.

What is in the dataset

The 356 open columns are released as data, under CC BY 4.0 or, for the columns derived from Wikipedia, CC BY-SA 4.0. The 52 columns built from licensed sources (CRSP, Compustat, I/B/E/S, and a few third-party series) are not redistributed: scripts rebuild them from your own access and check the result against our checksums. For each column, the codebook gives its source, the first date with data, a quality grade, whether its value is known before the open, and its license. A monthly version aggregates the daily data into 792 months.

Updates

The data are updated once a year, in the first quarter, when the year-end data are available. Each update is a new version with its own DOI; earlier versions stay available unchanged.
